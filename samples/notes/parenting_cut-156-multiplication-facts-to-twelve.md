## In one line
A presenter filling in a wall-mounted 12x12 grid, narrating the order she teaches multiplication facts in as she shades each cell.

## Core claim
Almost none of the 156 multiplication facts actually need memorising: if you teach the zero, commutative and identity properties first, then the 2s, 5s and 10s the child already half-knows, then the 3s, 9s and 11s as patterns, you are left with roughly twelve facts that genuinely require rote drill.

## Key points
- The commutative property is the main lever and is taught first and daily: knowing 3x7 hands you 7x3 free, which halves the grid before any memorising starts. The claim is that this is what collapses a child's "that's hundreds of problems" panic.
- The 2s, 5s and 10s are treated as prior knowledge to be activated rather than taught: doubling for the 2s, skip counting for the 5s, place value for the 10s. She claims this plus the properties clears about 70 facts inside week one.
- The 9s are taught as a two-part digit pattern (tens digit is one less than the multiplier, and the two digits sum to nine); the 11s as the repeated-digit pattern up to 11x9, deliberately said as "seven seven" rather than "seventy-seven" to keep it automatic rather than calculated.
- The 4s are derived as double the 2s and the 6s as double the 3s, leaving only the square numbers, the 12s and 8x7 as genuine memorisation. Flashcards appear only at this stage, ordered so each fact is immediately followed by its reverse.
- Two stated prerequisites: the child must already understand what multiplication means conceptually, and must be fluent in addition, or the derivation strategies collapse into guesswork.

## Verdict
SOLID BUT FAMILIAR. This is derived-facts teaching, which is the mainstream position in maths education and is essentially what the English national curriculum and most primary schemes already assume; the sequencing is clean and the "watch the number of unknown facts shrink" framing is a genuinely good motivational device, but nothing here is new. Note explicitly: this makes substantially the same core claim as the Build Math Minds subitizing video you also saved on the same day (jwnzYXeSpfM) - both argue that anchor facts (2s, 5s, 10s, 1s, 0s) plus commutativity plus doubling reduce the memorisation load to a handful, and both creators sell a course off the back of it. The difference is method, not claim: this one is a procedural fill-in-the-grid sequence that still ends in flashcards and songs, the other rejects rote drill in favour of visual reasoning. If you only act on one, they are not additive.

## Try this
- Print a blank 12x12 grid this weekend and work the video's order with him over the week: properties first, then 2s, 5s, 10s, shading each cell as it is secured and its mirror cell at the same time. The point is that he physically sees the unshaded count dropping - that is the part of this worth stealing.
- Rebuild your flashcard deck so every fact is immediately followed by its reverse (7x3 then 3x7, 8x4 then 4x8) for one week, then shuffle. Cheap change, tests whether he actually has commutativity or has just been told about it.
- Isolate the residue set - 6x7, 6x8, 7x8, 4x7, 7x7, 8x8 - and drill only those at Year 4 Multiplication Tables Check pace, six seconds per question, rather than re-running tables he already owns. If he is at or past MTC age this is the exact set that costs marks.

## Watch it anyway?
No. It is a woman filling in a wall-mounted grid; the grid adds nothing the ordered list above does not give you, and she narrates every step anyway. The only thing you would gain is the sight of the chart filling up, and you can reproduce that yourself with a printed grid and a highlighter.
