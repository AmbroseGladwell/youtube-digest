## In one line
Ryan Humiston coaching a cable-based arm session - curl and triceps variations, form fixes, frequency - as the free hook for his paid programmes.

## Core claim
Big arms come from high-volume, cable-heavy work done at long muscle lengths, progressed by adding sets rather than adding weight, on a dedicated arm day you can repeat two or three times a week.

## Key points
- Progress arms by adding sets, or an entire extra arm day, rather than chasing load; form degrades the moment you chase a number.
- Train through the stretched position: partials at length, leaning back on curls to shift peak tension into the lengthened bicep, not locking out, and flexing over the elbow as a fulcrum instead of "pushing out" on extensions.
- Cables and machines are preferred over free weights for arms because tension stays constant without contorting your form to get it.
- Arms tolerate much more frequency and volume than legs, so training them two or three times a week is claimed to be recoverable.
- If you cannot feel the target muscle, the cause is usually form or a secondary muscle taking over; constrain the joint (a band around the elbows on skull crushers, or a single-arm variation) instead of pushing through.

## Verdict
SOLID BUT FAMILIAR
The training principles line up reasonably with current hypertrophy thinking, but every one of them has been circulating in gym content for years and none will be new to anyone who already trains. The supporting reasoning is anecdote dressed as physiology — the forearm anecdote offered as proof that arms recover fast is a joke, not evidence — and the whole thing funnels toward four paid programmes, so read "Guaranteed" as marketing rather than a claim.

## Selling
Four paid training programmes at $20 each with lifetime access, plus his own app, into which he places a free version of this workout as the hook. Also invites comments and gym recommendations to drive engagement.

## Try this
1. On your next two arm sessions, keep every load identical to last time and add one set per exercise instead. Write the sets down so you can see volume went up while weight did not.
2. Swap one dumbbell curl and one dumbbell triceps extension for the cable version, and on the curl sit leaning slightly back so the hardest point falls where the bicep is stretched. Keep rests to about 60 seconds.
3. Take the one arm exercise you feel least, and do three sets of it with a band looped around your elbows so the joint cannot drift out of position.

## Watch it anyway?
No. The exercises are conventional and the useful adjustments — lean back on curls, keep elbows tucked, do not lock out, cable over dumbbell — survive being written down intact.
