## In one line
Veritasium explainer opening with an elephant killed by LSD in 1962, then animating biological scaling laws from shrews to cities, dissent included.

## Core claim
Biological traits scale with body mass as power laws rather than
proportionally, and because heart rate and lifespan scale as equal and
opposite quarter powers, they cancel out, leaving nearly every mammal with
roughly a billion heartbeats in its life.

## Key points
- Tusko the elephant was killed in 1962 by a dose of LSD calculated on the
  assumption that safe dosage scales linearly with mass. It does not: dosage
  tracks metabolic rate, which scales sublinearly.
- Kleiber's Law says metabolic rate scales as mass to the three quarters,
  not the two thirds that a surface-area argument predicts. West, Brown and
  Enquist explain the extra quarter by treating circulatory systems as
  space-filling fractals, whose effective surface dimension is three rather
  than two.
- Heart rate scales as mass to the minus one quarter and lifespan as mass to
  the plus one quarter, so their product is a constant. A shrew at 1200 beats
  per minute for 1.5 years and an elephant at 30 beats per minute for 65
  years both land near a billion.
- Humans are the sole outlier, at nearly three billion, and only since the
  mid-1800s. Sanitation and germ theory, not biology, bought the extra two.
- Cities follow their own scaling laws: infrastructure sublinear at about
  0.85, while wages, patents and also crime and disease run superlinear at
  about 1.15. No accepted theory explains those exponents yet.
- The video does not pretend this is settled. It gives airtime to Peter
  Dodds arguing the data does not support three quarters at all, and to the
  possibility that large and small mammals simply scale differently.

## Verdict
NOVEL. This is the rare saved video that is not repackaged advice: it is a
century-long scientific argument, presented with its own dissent intact and
without a tidy resolution. The billion heartbeats fact is the hook, but the
substance is the reasoning that gets there, and the honest ending is that
the research community is split. Nothing is being claimed about how you
should live, which is a large part of why it holds up.

## Selling
A Brilliant sponsor segment at the end, with a discount code, plus a link to
Geoffrey West's book "Scale" on which much of the video is based. Neither
distorts the argument, and the book is disclosed as the source rather than
smuggled in.

## Try this
1. Use the log-log plot idea with your son as maths enrichment: plot any two
   quantities that grow together and show that a straight line on log paper
   means one is a power of the other. It is a genuinely accessible way in to
   why exponents matter, and it beats another worksheet.
2. The roast timing rule is directly usable: doubling the weight of a joint
   means roughly 60% more time, not double, because cooking time scales as
   mass to the two thirds.

Beyond those two, nothing actionable. This is an explanation, not advice,
and it would be dishonest to manufacture a takeaway from it.

## Watch it anyway?
Yes, but only for one section. The fractal argument, where a two-dimensional
surface is folded until it effectively fills a three-dimensional volume, is
the load-bearing step in the whole theory and it is genuinely hard to hold in
your head from a written description. The animation carries information the
words do not. The rest of the video, including the billion heartbeats result
itself, reads perfectly well here.
