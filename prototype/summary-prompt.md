For the video at the given URL, produce a note in this exact structure.

## Video
Title: <exact video title>
Channel: <channel name>
Description: <the video description, trimmed to the first 400 characters.
If the description cannot be retrieved, write "unavailable" and do not
guess or reconstruct it from the transcript.>

## In one line
What this video IS, not what it argues. One sentence, 25 words at most.
Cover the format and the subject: who is talking, roughly how long, and what
territory it covers. This is the line I read when scanning, so it has to be
enough on its own for me to remember which video this was.
Good: "A 20 minute talking head walking through screen time rules age by
age, from babies to teenagers."
Bad: "An insightful exploration of modern parenting challenges."
Never start it with "This video" or "A video about".

## Core claim
One sentence. What is this video actually asserting? If it asserts nothing
and is pure vibes, say so plainly.

## Key points
3 to 5 bullets. Substance only. Strip the hook, the story, the restatement,
and the call to action.

## Verdict
One of: NOVEL / SOLID BUT FAMILIAR / RECYCLED / THIN / DUBIOUS
Then one or two sentences of reasoning. Be blunt. Most short-form content
is a repackaging of standard advice, and saying so is the most useful thing
you can tell me. If the claims contradict mainstream evidence, or the
creator is selling something the advice conveniently requires, flag it.

## Selling
Anything the creator is pitching: a course, a coaching programme, a
supplement, a book, an affiliate link, a "link in bio", their own app. Take
it from the transcript, and from the description when you have it. Write
"Nothing detected" if there is none. Note it here even when it does not
change the verdict, because the pattern across many notes is what I want
to see.

## Try this
1 to 3 things I could actually do in the next 7 days. Concrete and specific,
not principles. "Do a 10 minute phone-free wind-down with him before bed
on school nights" not "prioritise connection". If there is nothing
actionable, write "Nothing actionable" rather than inventing something.

I have a son and a daughter. Do not default to one of them. If a video's
advice fits one child's age or stage and not the other's, name that child.
If it applies to both, say so.

## Watch it anyway?
Yes or No, plus a reason. Default to No. Say Yes only if the visual IS the
claim: a physical technique I would perform wrong from a written description,
a demonstration whose outcome is the evidence, or a visual that carries
information the words do not. It is not enough that the video has visuals,
or that seeing it would be nicer, or that a demonstration merely illustrates
a point the text already made. If a competent written description would get
me to the same place, the answer is No. Assume I am trying to stop watching
these, and that a soft Yes costs me ten minutes I will not get back.

## Topic
Exactly one of the following. Use the guidance, not just the label.

parenting - raising my son and my daughter, family life, school,
  childhood. Where advice is age or stage specific, say which child it
  applies to rather than assuming it is the older one.
finance - money, investing, tax, saving, spending, pensions
business - work, career, professional skill, running things
fitness - training, nutrition, mobility, sleep, health habits
relationships - marriage, friendship, conflict, social skills
personal-growth - habits, productivity, motivation, self-management
interesting - genuinely worth knowing but with nothing to apply.
  Prefer this over unsorted for good content that is not actionable.
unsorted - only when you honestly cannot tell. This is a failure
  state, not a category, so use it rarely.

A video about money is finance even when the framing is career, and a
video about career decisions is business even when the reasoning is
financial. Ask what the video is actually about, not what it mentions.

3 to 6 lowercase tags for search.
